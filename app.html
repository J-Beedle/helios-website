About Helios Live

The Heliophysics System Observatory at any moment since March 2015: where each spacecraft was, the boundaries the solar wind set, and every mission's data a click away. Your browser reads it all straight from the public archives when you load a moment; no Helios server is involved.

Using it

Pick an event or a date and time (UTC) in the bar and press Load, or press Now for the present. The year, month and day change on their own, so the same day in another year is one change; the arrow keys step the day and the time, and a pasted date such as 2017-07-11 22:34 fills them all. Click any spacecraft for its data window, and Earth for the geomagnetic indices. Drag a window's bar to the top or a side of the screen to tile it; the green button opens it in a tab of its own. The down arrow in a window's bar saves its figure as a PNG, as it is drawn. The time bar under the view moves the whole fleet. The page's address always describes what is on screen, so a copied link opens the same view.

Now: real time

Now (or ?t=now in the address) shows the present and follows the clock, for watching space weather and for operations. The Now panel says when each feed was last fetched and how old its newest sample is, and marks a feed stale when that sample is more than 15 minutes old (four hours for the 3-hour Kp). Feeds refresh while the tab is open, no more than once a minute, and pause while it is hidden; the page reopens on the new present every two hours or so.

The solar wind is measured at L1: NOAA SWPC's real-time solar wind (RTSW, 1-minute, from whichever monitor SWPC has active; rows SWPC flags are left out). Each minute is carried to the bow-shock nose as a flat front moving along GSE X at its measured Vx, from the spacecraft's position (SWPC's ephemeris) to the Jelínek et al. (2012) nose for that minute's pressure; the panel gives the delay, and SWPC's own propagation to 32 RE beside it. The wind arriving at the nose sizes the drawn magnetopause and bow shock. Dynamic pressure is OMNI's 2×10⁻⁶ n V² nPa; the standoff is Shue et al. (1998), flagged when it is inside geosynchronous orbit (6.6 RE). CCMC's real-time SWMF run gives a second, model standoff.

Kp and the scales are NOAA SWPC's: the last 3-hour planetary Kp, the 1-minute estimated Kp (which sets the field lines and the oval when the page opens) and the latest NOAA R, S and G scales. Positions are SSCWeb's ephemeris, for the present the missions' predicted orbits; a spacecraft with no position for the present is listed, not drawn. Each spacecraft's region is the model's answer from the drawn boundaries, not a measurement.

The Sun, the particles and the outlook follow what NOAA SWPC's own dashboards lead with: the NOAA scales now, their 24-hour maximum and SWPC's forecast for today and the two days after; SWPC's alerts, watches and warnings in effect; GOES's X-ray flux with the latest flare, and its ≥10 MeV protons and ≥2 MeV electrons at geosynchronous orbit over six hours; SWPC's 3-day Kp forecast; Dst (WDC Kyoto's quick-look values, provisional); and OVATION's hemispheric auroral power. "Sun and aurora" shows SWPC's latest images (GOES SUVI, SOHO LASCO C3, OVATION and WSA-Enlil) once it is opened. Each section folds, and the panel can be set two columns wide.

GOES and SDO open in real time. A GOES window shows the field and integral particle fluxes from SWPC's primary and secondary feeds (through LASP's Space Weather Portal, minutes behind), with SWPC's real-time X-rays from that satellite and its differential proton and electron fluxes; it fetches again every two minutes and covers the last hour (its menu sets the length). SDO's window shows the observatory's latest frame of each band, fetched again every 15 minutes; drag the time bar back and it shows the Sun at that time instead.

Not available in real time: MMS science data (Level 2 files reach the archives weeks after the day), and the other missions' and OMNI's archive data, which follow by days to weeks. Their windows stay closed in this mode; pick a past moment for them.

What you are looking at

The view. OMNI's 1-minute solar wind sizes the magnetopause and the bow shock; the view names each model it draws. Frames come from MMS's ephemeris, or are computed here for moments it has not reached. Orbits are SSCWeb's. Field lines are traced through IGRF-14 and T89 at the moment's Kp; the auroral oval follows Starkov with the hour's AL.

MMS. FGM, FPI moments and spectra, HPCA, EDP and DSP: survey for four hours around the moment and burst within ten minutes of it, with electron, ion and HPCA distribution cuts on request.

Other missions. THEMIS and ARTEMIS, Cluster 1–4, GOES-16 to 19, ACE, Wind and DSCOVR, and OMNI. A GOES window has the satellite's field, its X-rays and the integral particle fluxes, and, for the last seven days, SWPC's differential proton and electron fluxes.

SDO. Click SDO, or the Sun on the Sun rung, for the Sun as SDO saw it: AIA's 193, 304, 171, 211, 131 and 94 Å, HMI's magnetogram and continuum, or four at once. The window follows the time bar: it fetches a strip of frames across the four hours (Helioviewer's renders of the SDO archive, about 300 kB each), shows the one nearest the time bar, and fetches the exact moment once you stop dragging. The download button saves the frame on screen as a 2048-px PNG from Helioviewer (in Quad, one file per channel).

Timing and accuracy. The numbers are the archives' current Level 2 versions, read at full precision where the archive allows and not checked by hand. A moment from the last few weeks opens with positions only until the missions publish their data.
