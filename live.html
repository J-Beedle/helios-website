About this prototype

Survey and burst data for any moment

The screens and tools are the demo's. Only where the data come from has changed: here every number is fetched when you press Load, for the moment you pick.

MMS. FGM survey B in GSE (shown as one-second averages, fetched in eight pieces side by side), FPI fast-survey ion and electron moments and omni spectra, and MEC's orbit, Kp, dipole tilt and attitude quaternions, all from CDAWeb's HAPI server. FPI's energy tables are the demo's, which HAPI does not serve (burst records alternate between two, by FPI's step-table parity). Burst FGM and FPI moments load for ±10 minutes around the moment, where MMS took burst data. Outside MMS's science regions FPI is off, so only B shows.

Electron distributions. A VDF cut fetches FPI-DES burst distributions within a second of its time, in HAPI's binary format, about 4 MB a second of data, with B from burst FGM and the electrons' bulk velocity at each record. GSE and GSM cuts turn the instrument frame with the probe's own MEC attitude, as Helios does. The cuts themselves are the demo's: geometric, on a 100 × 100 grid, with the same frames and options.

Other missions. THEMIS A, D and E, and ARTEMIS P1 and P2 at the Moon: FGM; ion and electron moments from the spin-resolution MOM product, with field-aligned temperatures; E from EFI's spin fits; and ESA reduced-mode spectra on Helios's energy tables. These are the products Helios reads. Cluster 1–4: FGM spin, CIS and PEACE prime parameters with Helios's checks (records outside physical bounds and one-record density dropouts are dropped), CIS composition (O⁺, He⁺, He⁺⁺), and the CIS ion spectrogram from CDAWeb (HIA on Cluster 1 and 3, CODIF H⁺ on Cluster 4; glitch cells and unswept half-sweeps removed, zero flux left blank), and PEACE's pitch-angle DEFlux from ESA's Cluster Science Archive, averaged over pitch angle into the electron spectrogram. Each loads when its window opens, ahead of any burst data still arriving. Instruments that were off that day are left out of the window.

The view. OMNI's 1-minute solar wind sizes the magnetopause and bow shock. GEO and SM come from MEC's quaternions. THEMIS, Cluster, ARTEMIS, the Moon, SDO, the Van Allen Probes, Geotail and the L1 monitors are SSCWeb's. The planets use JPL's approximate elements, the stars are turned to the day, and the all-sky imagers are Helios's site list for that month.

Solar wind and the ground. Clicking Earth opens the OMNI window with Helios's standard rows (|B|, Bz and By in GSM, speed, density, dynamic pressure, the Alfvén Mach number and the Shue et al. 1998 stand-off), with Bx, temperature, Ey, β, the magnetosonic Mach number, SYM-H, AE and PCN in the sidebar. ACE, Wind and DSCOVR open their own windows at the spacecraft's time, from the science products Helios uses and the browse products where those stop. In LEO, the globe marks the imagers that published a keogram that day (UCalgary's data-distribution listing for THEMIS, REGO, TREx RGB and SMILE), and clicking one opens its day's keogram with a line at the view's time. Each dark THEMIS camera's one-minute frame is laid on the globe at 110 km, as Helios's aurora layer does: UCalgary's hourly montages, placed pixel by pixel through each installation's skymap (reduced once from UCalgary's IDL files by helios_website_tools/prepare_asi_skymaps.py), each frame divided by its median sky, the median a translucent blue and brighter sky through green, inside the camera's 10° elevation circle. ⌘-scroll, pinch or the + / − buttons zoom the globe.

What it costs. About 20–30 MB per MMS probe for the four hours of survey, most of it FGM, plus up to 20–40 MB of burst data where MMS took it, and a few MB for the scene. The archives answer in a few seconds each; nothing is cached between visits yet.
